## Tier 1: Primary Candidates (Direct GitHub Contributors)

### Jerome Etienne — @jeromeetienne
- **Repos:** AR.js (15.8k ⭐), AR-js-org/AR.js maintainer
- **Expertise:** Web AR, markerless tracking, geospatial AR, city-scale AR
- **Why he's perfect:** Created the most-starred open-source Web AR library. His pivot from individual to org ownership of AR.js mirrors the podcast's theme of collaborative perception.
- **Episode fit:** Episode 1 (latency in web-based AR), Episode 3 (geospatial interfaces)

### Hiuk Kim — @hiukim
- **Repos:** MindAR (2.7k ⭐), Unity WebAR Foundation, MindAR Studio
- **Expertise:** ML-based Web AR, TensorFlow.js tracking, face/image/body tracking
- **Why he's perfect:** Personally maintains the only actively updated web AR SDK with commercial-grade features. His Rust→JS→ML journey embodies the perceptual engineering story.
- **Episode fit:** Episode 1 (ML for latency compensation), Episode 3 (non-visual interfaces via hand/body tracking)

### Valve VR Community — ValveSoftware/openvr maintainers
- **Repos:** OpenVR SDK (6.6k ⭐)
- **Expertise:** Motion smoothing, interleaved reprojection, VSync timing, playspace tracking
- **Why they're perfect:** They live inside the perceptual latency problem daily. Issues #1012, #1729, and #1917 are master classes in the gap between spec intent and perceptual reality.
- **Episode fit:** Episode 1 (the 20ms rule, motion smoothing ethics)

### Igalia / Mozilla Reality Team — @svillar, @NathanaelA, @Utopiah
- **Repos:** Wolvic (963 ⭐), Firefox Reality
- **Expertise:** WebXR spec implementation, spatial navigation, audio layer architecture
- **Why they're perfect:** They build the browser layer that's supposed to make spatial perception accessible. Their issues (#992, #1110, #1180) reveal exactly where the spec falls short of the promise.
- **Episode fit:** Episode 2 (spatial audio and the spec gap), Episode 3 (interfaces beyond the flat screen)
